# Channel tasks

Every growth engine's channel specialists run the same seven tasks. Krish asked for each one as a task in its own right, applied to every social channel his engines run (2026-10-30). This file is the one definition. Each engine keeps only a mapping table that says which of its specialists runs which task, plus the five-step loop below with its own numbers. It never keeps a copy of these contracts.

The tasks began as seven Instagram growth prompts Krish saved on 2026-10-30. They are rewritten here for one change: a channel is never the goal. No engine's North Star counts followers. Every output names the North Star signal it serves.

## The rules every task follows

1. **The North Star signal.** Every idea, hook, caption, test and plan names the leading signal it is meant to move: sends, saves, follows from the right people, tracked link clicks, subscribes, orders or good meetings. It never names likes or reach alone. An output with no signal is refused.
2. **Official apart from speculation.** A claim about how a platform ranks or distributes posts is labelled `official` only when it comes from the platform's own pages (creator centre, help centre, engineering or product announcement), with its link and the date it was read. Anything else is labelled `speculation`. A claim with no source or no date is dropped.
3. **Live inputs only.** The placeholders (niche, audience, goal, capacity, followers) are filled from the engine's own canon and live readings at run time, never from memory or an older copy.
4. **Nothing goes out.** Every task drafts, ranks or proposes. Posting, sending, spending and any change to a profile, bio, pin or setting keep the engine's approval route. A task output is never approval.
5. **Never copy a creator.** A trend or a format is adapted to the venture's own product, voice and audience; another creator's words, footage or exact structure are not reused.

## The loop from today to the North Star

Each engine runs this loop each week, with its own numbers, and writes it into its own engine document.

1. **Gap.** This week's ladder target minus the measured number, in the opening line the Engines section asks for (`X of Y, U-n, short by Z`). An unmeasured number is U4 and measuring it is the first job.
2. **Contribution.** Split the gap across the engine's channels by last week's measured results. With no baseline, split it evenly and label that split an assumption.
3. **Leading signal.** Name, for each channel, the signal that comes before the number (for example sends per reach, tracked link clicks, subscribes from a post).
4. **Tasks.** Choose the week's mix of the seven tasks so each channel's leading signal moves. At U3 and above the volume rises to the engine's policy bounds, as the Engines section says.
5. **Review.** Read each post and test at 24 hours, 72 hours and 7 days against its own channel median, keep or kill, and record what won and what Krish declined where the next draft reads it. The check-in asks him whether the plan is on track.

## What an engine keeps

- A mapping table: specialist (or job), channels, the task ids it runs, its leading signal.
- The loop above, filled with its own ladder, channels and signals.
- A deterministic check that every task id it names is one of the seven.

Mindmake's own engine is outside the first round: its number is good meetings from warm paths, and none of its specialists owns a social channel (Krish, 2026-10-30).
