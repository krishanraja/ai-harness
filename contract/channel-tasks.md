# Channel tasks

Krish asked for each of seven growth prompts to be a task in its own right for the channel specialists in his growth engines, and then for each repository to know what it needs to accomplish rather than follow a box-standard template, because each venture needs very different outcomes (Ruling, Krish, 2026-10-30). So this file holds only what every engine shares: the rules below, a starting set of seven tasks, and the loop. What a task accomplishes, and which engines run it, belongs to each engine's own repository.

The source, with his words and the seven original prompts quoted, is `docs/proposals/2026-10-30-channel-tasks.md`. This file supersedes the prompts' wording. They are rewritten here for one change: a channel is never the goal. No engine's North Star counts followers. Every output names the North Star signal it serves.

## The rules every task follows

1. **The North Star signal.** Every idea, hook, caption, test and plan names the leading signal it is meant to move: sends, saves, follows from the right people, tracked link clicks, subscribes, orders or good meetings. It never names likes or reach alone. An output with no signal is refused.
2. **Official apart from speculation.** A claim about how a platform ranks or distributes posts is labelled `official` only when it comes from the platform's own pages (creator centre, help centre, engineering or product announcement). Otherwise it is `speculation`, ranked below official sources as `evidence-research.source-hierarchy` ranks them. Its link and read date follow `truth-and-freshness.record-source-and-retrieval`; a claim without them is dropped.
3. **Live inputs.** The placeholders (niche, audience, goal, capacity, followers) are filled by `truth-and-freshness.prefer-live-state-for`: the engine's own canon and live readings at run time.
4. **Drafts only.** Every task drafts, ranks or proposes; `authority.never-publish-post-send` governs anything that goes out, and a task output is never approval.
5. **Never copy a creator.** A trend or a format is adapted to the venture's own product, voice and audience; another creator's words, footage or exact structure are not reused.

## Who owns the words

The tasks produce choices, not final copy. Under `collision-rules.krish-content-marketer-owns`, `krish-content-marketer` owns the angle and conversion structure the idea bank, hooks and plan choose between. Under `collision-rules.krish-voice-owns-final`, `krish-voice` owns the final prose of anything in Krish's own name, so a pick from `reel-script` or `caption-set` on his personal account passes the voice pass before release. On a venture's brand account, final words follow that venture's own voice canon. The 5 hooks and 5 captions are the alternatives these owners and the engine's creative review choose from; neither task overrides them.

## An engine's own brief comes first

For every task a specialist runs, the engine writes one sentence saying what that task accomplishes on that channel for that venture: who it is for, what counts as a win, and what this venture never does there. A store's idea bank and a publication's idea bank are different tasks with one name. A sentence that would read the same in another engine, or for another channel in the same engine, is a template, and the engine's own check refuses it.

An engine reshapes a task to fit, and adds its own task when its outcome needs one this list lacks. It drops a task from a channel only with evidence that the task cannot serve it there: a channel rule it would break, a ruling, or a measured result. A drop is recorded with that evidence where the engine keeps its brief, and is reversible. The seven are a floor for thinking, not a quota.

**Krish's time.** The tasks add no new asks of their own. Profile and creative changes, plans and check-ins reach him through the engine's existing route, one ask per engine at a time (Engines rule 4 in `contract/north-star-metric.md`), batched with the work they belong to.

## The loop from today to the North Star

Each engine runs this loop each week, with its own numbers, and writes it into its own engine document.

1. **Gap.** The opening line Engines rule 1 sets, from the engine's own ladder.
2. **Contribution.** Split the gap across the engine's channels by last week's measured results. With no baseline, split it evenly and label that split an assumption.
3. **Leading signal.** Name, for each channel, the signal that comes before the number (for example sends per reach, tracked link clicks, subscribes from a post).
4. **Tasks.** Choose the week's mix of tasks so each channel's leading signal moves, at the volume Engines rule 2 sets.
5. **Review.** Read each published post and test against its own channel median, at the windows the engine's channel rules already set, and keep or kill. Record what won and what Krish declined where the next draft reads it. Check in on the plan as Engines rule 5 says.

## What counts as done

A green check on an engine's brief is only the floor. The tasks are working when the leading signal for a channel moves on published posts, read at review, and the North Star number follows. A week of drafts with no published post and no reading is reported as that, never as progress.

## What an engine keeps

- Its brief: for each specialist, the channels, its leading signal, and for each task it runs, what that task accomplishes there, with any dropped task and its evidence.
- The loop above, filled with its own ladder, channels and signals.
- A deterministic check that refuses an unknown task, a missing or one-word outcome, and the same outcome on two specialists.
