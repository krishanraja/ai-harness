## Shared distinction

These are purpose and format playbooks. They do not prove that a channel is active, reachable, publishing on a stated cadence, or still using the same hosts and format. Run the status gate in `SKILL.md` before production.

Editorial work should demonstrate a point of view and usefulness rather than smuggle in an offer CTA. Use a verified, current operator artifact wherever possible.

## Techonomic

**Mandate:** investigate how the digital world gets paid for as attention, content, distribution, access, and the open web change.

- Audience: operators, founders, publishers, media commercial leaders, and strategically curious builders.
- Stance: interrogative and investigative, with a committed view earned through the inquiry.
- Characteristic question: who pays for this, where does value move, and what mechanism changes when the shift lands?
- Native form: durable essay, normally long enough to trace a mechanism and counterargument. Confirm the current publishing format live.
- Opening: a structural framing, historical inversion, or economic tension; never generic AI trend context.
- Closing: a forward-looking verdict or reusable economic lens; never "time will tell."
- Strongest artifact: a commercial pattern, experiment, or P&L reality Krish has observed directly.
- Antagonist: incentives or a failing model, not a named villain.
- Helpful test: the reader can apply the money question to their own corner of the digital economy.

AdFixus may appear as evidence where relevant, but cookie mechanics or identity infrastructure must not shrink the whole subject into a disguised product argument.

## The Builder Economy

**Mandate:** explore the people using AI to move from creator or operator to builder, and reach the human reason underneath the stated reason for the build.

- Audience: builders, would-be builders, creators crossing into building, and people looking for permission plus a usable first step.
- Stance: energetic, warm, scrappy, generous, and capable of dry humour.
- Characteristic move: surface why -> strategic why -> human why. Ask warmly; the guest should feel seen, not interrogated.
- Native form: builder-to-builder conversation with cutdowns built around the most retellable human or practical moment. Verify launch and publishing state live.
- Opening: enter through the build, origin scene, or strange decision, not the guest's CV.
- Closing: momentum and an accessible handhold that makes the listener feel able to begin.
- Strongest artifact: the guest's lived story, met by Krish's own builder experience.
- Antagonist: the gate that says building requires permission, credentials, or a large team.
- Helpful test: inspiration is paired with how the first imperfect version actually happened.

Do not turn the conversation into a stack tour. The third turn of the why is more valuable than a list of tools.

## Signal & Noise

**Mandate:** stress-test AI and media conventional wisdom in real time and separate operational signal from hype and doom.

- Audience: media, adtech, and AI insiders who recognise both vendor hype and elegant outside-the-arena pessimism.
- Stance: adversarial toward ideas, generous toward people, and comfortable with an honest unresolved tension.
- Characteristic moves: the no-BS call, deliberate devil's advocate, and a what-if that reveals mechanism rather than merely provoking.
- Argument order: first steelman the strongest version of the consensus and credit what it gets right; only then apply devil's advocate and the no-BS call.
- Native form: dialogic audio with genuine build-and-counter rhythm. Verify current hosts, feed, and format live.
- Opening: enter mid-argument through the spiky claim or contrarian question; no ceremonial welcome.
- Closing: sharpen the disagreement and give the listener a defensible lens, even when consensus is not reached.
- Strongest artifact: friction between informed lived views, including Krish's production experience.
- Antagonist: the hype/doom binary, not the people caught inside it.
- Helpful test: the listener can identify signal independently during the next hype cycle.

Contrarianism without evidence is noise. A what-if that only sounds edgy fails the channel.

## Mindmaker Live

**Mandate:** keep a busy executive oriented to the few AI developments that change a decision and explain why each matters.

- Audience: senior leaders accountable for AI but unable to read the entire firehose.
- Stance: calm, teaching, selective, and candid about uncertainty.
- Characteristic move: observable update -> operator so-what -> decision implication.
- Native form: a concise briefing with clear sections. Confirm the current section names, medium, and cadence live.
- Opening: orient to the most load-bearing change immediately.
- Closing: name what to watch or the one move worth making next.
- Strongest artifact: current first-party news filtered through Krish's operating judgment.
- Antagonist: undifferentiated news volume and AI-literacy theatre.
- Helpful test: the reader is more oriented and less anxious, with a concrete decision implication.

Every included item must be dated and sourced. A headline without a so-what is aggregation, not Mindmaker Live. Do not preserve historical fixed section counts or cadence as permanent instructions.

## Repurposing without duplication

A single source may become multiple editorial assets only when each earns a distinct treatment:

- Techonomic traces the economic mechanism.
- The Builder Economy finds the human motive and usable beginning.
- Signal & Noise stress-tests the consensus.
- Mindmaker Live prioritises the immediate executive implication.

Copy-pasting paragraphs across channels fails even if logos, openings, or lengths differ. Record the shared source, then create a channel-specific angle, structure, evidence emphasis, and audience payoff.
