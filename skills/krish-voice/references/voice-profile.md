# Krish voice profile

Version 1.4, 31 October 2026. Status: **merged on Krish's instruction on 31 Oct ("merge everything to main"). One branch is open (voiceover). Any change lands only on his yes; his markup of a live draft is the expected trigger.**

`voice-doctrine.md` says how to write as Krish. This profile shows how he actually writes and talks, measured from his own published words with the source for every claim. Read it next to the doctrine when drafting anything in his name, and use the numbers to check a draft.

Where the two agree, the profile cites the doctrine and does not repeat it. Where they disagreed, Krish ruled (see Rulings); on anything not ruled, the doctrine wins.

## Core: what holds everywhere

1. **He leads with the point, often as a verdict.** "Contrarian take: AI Slop is actually human slop." (LinkedIn, 28 May 2026). "Another day, another "my AI is better than yours" bro flex." (LinkedIn, 29 Jan 2025). Agrees with the doctrine's "no warm-up paragraph".
2. **Then he reframes the question.** "So the question isn't whether agentic browsers happen. They will happen. The question is whether you enter it by design or by default." (LinkedIn, 24 Oct 2025). In 2021, live: "it's not about building something specific for Australia, it's about turning what you have into a local story" (Scaleup).
3. **He proves things with his own time in the trade, specifically.** "I was one of the first programmatic traders at Microsoft in 2011 and 'automated trading' was hilariously counterproductive and manual." "I had to literally run seminars at Nine assuring sellers they still had a job in 2014." (tech0nomic, 13 Mar 2025). Agrees with the doctrine's "someone who has done the work".
4. **He puts himself down before he puts anything else down.** "As usual, I took barely any decent pictures, so I snipped some out of the podcasts, and edited myself in" (LinkedIn, 30 Apr 2026). "studying at the same time as starting in a new business was potentially not the smartest decision I ever made for balance!" (LinkedIn, 7 May 2021). Live: "At the time I was just being lazy to be frank" (Scaleup, 2021). Agrees with the doctrine's "self-deprecating when earned".
5. **His irony is short and dry, and it follows a long sentence.** "A new wave of businesses promising fast growth during a stagnant economic moment (but secretly mining your data)? Couldn't imagine it." "If it does it'll be far worse." (tech0nomic, 27 Mar 2025).
6. **He lets enthusiasm show, in caps and exclamation marks.** "direct sales are BACK BABY!" (tech0nomic, 13 Mar 2025). "Pumped! Just ran my biggest AI cohort yet ... and it was FUN!" (LinkedIn, 6 May 2026). 36% of his posts end on an exclamation mark. Ruling 4 places it: on camera he keeps this energy, even when arguing; in writing he stays dry, and on LinkedIn "fun" (ruling 5) is the ceiling.
7. **He drops the subject when the feeling comes first.** "Pumped!", "Possible wrap!", "Loved this one!", "Incredibly relieved to have passed my Harvard Business Online courses!" Agrees with the doctrine's dropped subject pronouns.
8. **His dash is a spaced hyphen, and his asides go in brackets.** "Here's to telling random people you like what they do - it leads to good things!" (LinkedIn, 26 Jun 2026). His own writing carries 5 to 8 spaced hyphens and 2 to 4 brackets per 1,000 words. Generic rewrites of the same pieces carried none. Never an em dash (house rule).
9. **He writes the way he would say it.** He uses 22 to 33 contractions per 1,000 words, against about 4 in a polished rewrite. Live, he uses 53. He also uses casual coinages: "relatable AF" (2021), "Amirite? Or amiwrong?" (2026), "elbow grease".
10. **He thanks people by name, and means it.** 29 of his closing lines thank someone. He credits the room, the hosts and the team, often naming them.

### LinkedIn post
- Opens on the news or the feeling, never on context: "Pumped!", "And so an era ends.", "Who better to review Cannes with than the guy who roasts CEOs at Cannes for a living?"
- Middle: one argument, often a reframe, sometimes a numbered list of takeaways.
- Closes on thanks, an invitation or an exclamation: "Love to hear different perspectives!", "Link in the comments!", "comment or DM me! It's what I love to do."
- Since 2025 he talks to the reader more ("you" up from 9 to 14) and uses brackets for asides.
- About one post in six used Unicode bold headers or 👉 and ✅ bullets. Ruling 3 retires them: one emoji for feeling at most.

### Long-form
- Shorter sentences than his posts (about 13 words), more of them short, and "you" at 15 to 16.
- Builds on his own history and a named example: "An example: Subconscious AI." (tech0nomic, 12 May 2025).
- Asks for plain answers on the reader's behalf: "ask for a plain-English answer so you can understand its validity" (same piece).
- Keeps the irony and the occasional all-caps verdict.

### Live conversation
- The warmest, most hedged and most personal register. "I think", "I guess" and "sort of" come about 20 times per 1,000 words, and almost half his sentences open with "Yeah", "So", "Look" or "And".
- Hedges and the "Yeah, so" openings belong to speech. They never transfer to writing.
- What does transfer: luck and gratitude ("I've been a very lucky lad"), a single number that carries the point ("16 of the top 20 brands in Australia don't exist outside of Australia"), and the reframe.

### Spoken script
Measured on 14 takes of 7 pieces (12 to 30 Oct 2026): what he changed from the script on camera. Per 100 script words he swaps about 18, adds about 22 and cuts about 3. The 90-second cuts move more than the 5-minute ones (50 against 42 changes per 100 words).

- **He keeps the length and changes the words.** He rarely drops a scripted line, so length is not the problem; the wording is. Write the script near the length it already is.
- **He says it plainer.** "dollars" became "bucks", "firms" became "about the companies", "looking" became "having a look", "polished" became "really nice", "kinds" became "types". Write the casual word first.
- **He adds a short aside, often on "which is".** "Qwen, which is...", "which is OpenAI, now retired", "which is fair enough", "and you can guess who". Leave room for one aside where a name or a number needs explaining.
- **He tells his own part of the story.** On Wed 21 Oct (Muse vs Grok) he rewrote most of the script around the scam email he received ("for me unfortunately it was a bit of a dodgy looking email"). When a piece has his own experience in it, script the beats and let him say it.
- **He drops scripted throat-clearing.** "I have been following how money is changing", "I have been reading", "I am Krish" and "tell me in the comments" were left out.
- **Later pieces moved more, and the cause is not settled.** Mon 12 Oct was near word for word (10 to 16 changes per 100 words); later pieces ran 30 to 90. Either the scripts drifted from how he talks, or the scripts on file were edited after he read them (see Limits). From 31 Oct 2026 every new take is compared with the exact script by its hash, so later numbers can settle it; the 12 to 30 Oct numbers above cannot, and the rise is not a finding.

Limits: some swaps are the transcriber mishearing him ("Krish" heard as "Chris", "Qwen" as "Quinn"); the dated script on file may be a later edit than the one he read, which inflates the counts; and contractions are not settled, since the transcriber may write out ones he said. 19 takes had no matching script and are left out; the Drive file names each with its reason. The comparison of script and take (Stage 1 of the plan) is done, and now runs on every take as it is placed, against the exact script whose hash the piece's record holds (content-engine place-takes). Content-engine's Sunday learning compiler proposes what repeats across three pieces onto Krish's list (Krish, 2026-10-31: "start the weekly voice learner too", then "build the learner proposals onto my list"). If its proposals stop arriving, read this section as a snapshot of 31 Oct 2026.

## How it has moved

- **2020 to 2021:** warm, grateful, career-milestone posts, with lots of "I", "we" and thanks. "It's funny the perspective you miss when you're in the day to day." (20 Nov 2020).
- **2022 to 2024:** fewer posts, more "we" and company voice, and fewer contractions (17).
- **2025 to 2026:** the commentator voice. Contrarian openers, reframes and more "you", with irony and asides back. This is the register to aim for now.

## Rulings (Krish, 31 Oct 2026)

Rulings 1 to 5 settle the five places where v1 found the evidence and the doctrine disagreeing; ruling 6 settles Jonny's branch; rulings 7 and 8 settle how a script closes and how much it asks a listener to hold.

1. **Rhetorical questions:** allowed on LinkedIn only, sparingly. Banned in articles, scripts and emails.
2. **Calls to action:** "Humble, sincere, curious and observational".
3. **Formatting:** one emoji for feeling, on LinkedIn only. No emoji bullets and no Unicode bold anywhere.
4. **Energy:** dry in writing; on camera, his natural spoken energy, including when arguing.
5. **Voice by channel:** "linkedin needs to remain first person and sincere, fun, insightful, humble, curious and observational. makeyourmindup more like a professional media channel".

6. **Jonny's emails:** "jonny just needs to mimic my email style".
7. **The script close:** one short beat saying the one thing he would do, in his first person, never a list of orders ("the last paragraph of all my scripts still sounds a bit bossy and preachy", then "Yes, replace it"). "Here's what I'd do with this info if I were you" is retired. Rule in content-engine (`SCRIPT_CLOSE`).
8. **A slow listener follows every script, heard once:** "I still feel like all my video scripts are a little bit hard to follow", then "Yes, a hard rule". Short sentences alone do not do it: what a listener can hold does. The limits live only in content-engine's `LISTENING` rule (krishanraja/content-engine#307); read them there, never from here.

Still open: the voiceover register. It needs examples from Krish, or his words, before it is written down.

| His approved taste for Make Your Mind Up's spoken scripts: a read-aloud rule he keeps from measured changes | content-engine's learning proposals (scope `spoken:*`, venture publication) | his keep or reword on his list, the same Taste route | content-engine's Scripts step (`scripts/voice/spoken_rules.py`), the only writer of spoken scripts |

The last two rows are not doctrine. They are his approved taste for one venture, which the canon already lets a drafter read live on his yes (Taste, in the rendered canon); this file and `voice-doctrine.md` stay the doctrine and change only by reviewed pull request. Each approved rule has one home, its store: while it lives there, the store governs and this profile does not restate it. A measured pattern never becomes a rule on its own: a measurement proposes, and only his yes or his own words decide. When a kept rule should hold for every venture, it is proposed here as a doctrine change by pull request, and on his yes it moves here and its store entry is retired. Each growth repository reads the profile live and keeps no copy: content-engine in its drafting steps and voice score, growth-makeyourmindup in its post writer, and growth-mindmake through the approved rules on its directive and the Jonny row of `SKILL.md`'s safeguards.

## How to use it

- Drafting: read the Core and the register you are writing in, then write. Do not imitate the quoted lines; they show a pattern, not phrasing to reuse.
- Checking: `node evals/voice/fingerprint.mjs score <draft.txt> --register linkedin_post|long_form` gives a distance from 0 (as much like him as his typical piece) to 1. The test on 31 Oct 2026: on 10 held-out pieces of his, his original scored closer to him than a generic rewrite of the same piece 9 times out of 10, and 10 of 10 once the agent-voice pieces were taken out of the corpus. The rewrites were written by the model that built the test, and the measures were chosen after a first set of 10 failed at 5 of 10. Treat the score as a signal, not a verdict.
- Adding to it: a new piece of evidence goes in the corpus, then the fingerprint is rebuilt with `node evals/voice/fingerprint.mjs build <corpus.json>`. A change to this file is a suggestion with its evidence, and it lands only on Krish's yes.
