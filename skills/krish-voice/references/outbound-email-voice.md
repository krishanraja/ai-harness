# Outbound email voice

Use only after `krish-content-marketer` has locked the recipient, mode, argument, evidence, sequence job, and CTA.

## Shared voice

Write as if Krish is speaking directly to one person. Start at the point. Use contractions, specific commercial nouns and numbers, varied sentence lengths, and occasional natural fragments. Keep genuine conviction when the evidence supports it; do not polish it into corporate language.

Do not invent familiarity, recipient research, proof, urgency, customer outcomes, or first-person experience. Use the recipient's authorised language where it proves listening.

## Cold mode

Keep the first step compressed and conversational. A subject should feel like a human note, not a campaign slogan. Use one observation, one problem, one relevance/proof signal, and one low-friction question or offer. Avoid product lists, two questions, compliment openings, apology, and “checking in.”

Follow-ups must contribute something new. The final bump is shortest and emotionally unburdened.

## Named-prospect mode

Longer writing is allowed when context and usefulness earn it. Lead with their world. Use short, descriptive inline section anchors when several distinct topics matter. Let each section move from market/company context to what Krish is seeing to one authorised outcome.

Sell a useful link by naming the time cost and what the recipient will gain. A low-pressure line can state that the asset is useful even if they never buy, but only when the asset genuinely stands alone. Layered asks are acceptable only after the note has earned them.

Length is not the failure; repeated ideas and throat-clearing are. Remove any paragraph that adds no new information.

## Sender persona and signatures

Confirm the actual sender persona before drafting. Do not infer a colleague's voice from title alone. If a non-Krish persona lacks authorised examples, ask for the distinguishing posture or deliver a provisional version labelled as such.

For Instantly, end with the approved platform signature variable rather than writing a manual signature. For Gmail/manual drafting, omit a duplicate signature when the account auto-appends it. Verify the exact platform convention at execution time.

## Final smell test

Rewrite when the draft contains generic warm-up, a compliment opener, “I wanted to reach out,” vague “solutions/landscape,” inflated adjectives, multiple questions, repeated CTA, raw unexplained link, or a sentence Krish would not say aloud.

Check that:

- the first sentence is the point;
- one human detail or turn of phrase appears only where natural;
- the proof and recipient detail are authorised;
- the email's length matches its mode and every paragraph earns its place;
- the final CTA is specific and low-friction for the relationship;
- no send, schedule, upload, or campaign action is implied by writing approval.
