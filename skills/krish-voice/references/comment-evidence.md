# Comment evidence

Version 1, 11 November 2026. What Krish actually wrote when he commented on other people's posts, set against the assistant drafts he replaced. This file is evidence. The rules it led to are in `humour.md` ("Comments on someone else's post"), and only Krish's rulings there bind.

## Where it came from

Two sessions on 11 November 2026 drafted comments for Krish. He replaced most of the drafts and posted his own.

- **Heartside**, Instagram, the brand account `@yourdoghasnotes`. The aim was to reach US dog owners with comments that sound like a person joining the conversation, with no pitch and no link. Nine Krish-authored strings were verified in the account's own comment activity across eight posts (two are variants on one post), plus one earlier Krish-authored benchmark.
- **Make Your Mind Up and Krish's X.** Instagram `@makeyourmind.up`, YouTube `@makeyourmind-up` and X `@TheRealKrisho`. Of 20 drafts he reported posting, 11 could be read back. He changed 7 (5 replaced, 2 partly edited) and posted 4 unchanged.

The session exports are the source: "Krish voice corpus export: contextual social comments" (Heartside) and "Krish Raja's voice in outbound social comments" v1 (makeyourmindup), both dated 11 November 2026.

## How to read it

- **Krish** is his published wording, verbatim, including spelling, case and emoji. "bit" in H8 may be a typo for "bite". It is kept as it is, and it is not a rule.
- **Draft** is assistant-written. It was replaced, so it is never positive evidence of his voice.
- In a partial edit only the changed words are his. In the four unchanged posts the words are the assistant's, and they show only what he chose.
- "Move" is an assistant's reading of his choice. Except where a quote is given, he did not explain his jokes.
- A posted comment proves his taste. It does not prove reach, follows or sales, and nothing here measured them.
- These examples are disclosed. They can be regression cases, but not a held-out test.

## What the 16 cases show

Counted from the 16 cases where the words are his (9 Heartside, 7 makeyourmindup and X; the two H2 variants count once, using the later one, and partial edits count only the changed words).

- **The job varies.** Name, title or product puns 3 (H4, H6, H7). A first-person reaction or comparison 4 (H5, H8, P1, P3). Speaking as a character in the scene 2 (H1, P6). A real question 1 (H2). Warmth with no joke 1 (H3). Escalation 1 (P5). A resigned answer 1 (P4). A near-future scene 1 (P2). A reaction to the visual 1 (P7). A longer premise 1 (H9).
- **Length is not the difference.** His median is 11.5 words, against 11 for the 13 drafts he replaced. His range is wider: 1 to 28 words, against 6 to 21.
- **The drafts' premise was the tell.** Six of the 13 replaced drafts invented an off-screen job, payment or paperwork premise (location shoots, royalties, the chef, forensics, a payment plan, a finance purchase order). None of his replacements did. His one job premise, H9, was the job the dog was visibly doing.
- **The drafts had one register.** All 8 Heartside drafts were lower case and flatly deadpan ("i'm furious."). 14 of his 16 start with a capital, and the register shifts by post: one word in capitals 3 times (SLAY, EVERY DAY, HUMANS), an emoji in 3 of the 9 Heartside lines and none of the 7 others, "tbh", "bro", "ain't" and one swear word once each.
- **He stays with what is on screen.** Colour, pose, lips, eating, the hair the post is about, the interviewer's face. A caption detail that pulls away from the scene (H3's haircut) was dropped.
