# Methods by category

Make about ten ideas from three methods, each from a different category. Using three methods from one category gives ten versions of one idea. Record the method's id on each idea (`method` in the sheet); `scripts/validate_sheet.py` reads the ids from the headings below.

## Reframe

Change what the audience thinks the situation is.

### `invert-the-default`

Take the thing everyone in the category treats as fixed and flip it. If every brand shows the happy result, show the moment just before it; if every invite asks for time, offer to give some back. Keep the flip only if the audience's tension still drives it.

### `change-the-hero`

Move the centre of the story to someone the category ignores: the person on the receiving end, a bystander, the object itself. Ask what the tension looks like from that seat and what they would want to say.

### `rename-the-problem`

Give the obstacle in the insight a plain new name the audience will repeat. A named problem can be shared; an unnamed one stays private. Test the name by whether someone could use it in a sentence about their own week.

### `scale-shift`

Move the idea up or down by orders of magnitude: one second instead of one year, one person instead of a market, every day instead of once. Keep only shifts that make the tension sharper.

## Borrow

Bring a structure in from somewhere else.

### `foreign-format`

Lift a format from a different world (a weather report, a court ruling, a product recall, a sports commentary) and pour the insight into it. The format must carry meaning, not decoration.

### `ritual-hijack`

Attach the idea to something the audience already does on a schedule: a weekly habit, a seasonal moment, a daily routine. The ritual supplies the reason to act now.

### `cultural-collision`

Put the venture's world next to a current conversation the audience already follows and find where the two genuinely meet. Drop it if the link needs explaining.

### `analogy-transfer`

Find a domain that solved the same tension and carry its solution across. Name the source domain in the idea's mechanic so the reader sees the move.

## Provoke

Stretch the premise until something new falls out.

### `exaggerate-to-absurd`

Push the tension to its most extreme honest version and keep the part that is still true. Useful where the venture's taste allows humour; the humour engine reads the result where the adapter allows it.

### `remove-the-obvious`

List what every idea in this space must contain, take one essential element away, and see what has to replace it. The replacement is often the idea.

### `what-if-forbidden`

Write down what the venture would never do, then ask what a safe, honest version of that would look like. Stop at anything that breaks the floor, the claims gate or consent.

### `constraint-first`

Impose a hard limit (no words, one frame, one sentence, no product on screen) and make the idea work inside it. The limit forces a mechanic instead of a message.

## Participate

Make the audience part of the idea.

### `give-them-a-role`

Hand the audience a job inside the idea: choose, vote, finish the sentence, send it to the one person it describes. The role is the share mechanic.

### `make-it-a-tool`

Turn the insight into something useful the audience can take away and use: a check, a template, a calculator, a ready-made line. A tool earns the forward that an ad does not.

### `proof-in-public`

Run the claim as a visible test the audience can watch or join, with the result shown whatever it is. Only where the result can be measured and reported honestly.

### `invite-the-skeptic`

Address the person most likely to disagree and give them the floor. The idea is the response the venture gives in front of everyone.

## Measure

For a stall diagnosed as `measurement`. Every idea from this category has `idea_level: measurement`.

### `instrument-first`

Design the smallest piece of work whose only job is to make the number readable: a tracked link, a code, a question at checkout. The idea is what makes people use the instrument.

### `one-variable-test`

Pair two versions that differ in one thing the insight predicts matters, sent to comparable audiences, with the reading date fixed in advance.

### `ask-directly`

Put one plain question to the people who already acted and record the answers as counts and themes. The idea is the reason they would answer.
