Use for cold sequences and high-context named-prospect outreach. Final prose belongs to `krish-voice`.

## Required brief

Lock the venture/offer, sender persona, recipient role and seniority, company context, current trigger, recipient problem, approved proof, available asset/link, sequence position, region, suppression constraints, and intended next action. Ask one high-signal question when a missing answer would materially change the route; do not manufacture a generic email to avoid asking.

## Choose the mode

### Cold sequence

Use when the recipient is truly cold and the sequence operates at volume. The strategic job is to earn attention and a low-friction response, not explain the whole product. Use one problem, one relevance signal, at most one proof point, and one ask. Keep the argument compact enough to scan quickly.

### Named-prospect / warm / trigger-led

Use when Krish has specific recipient context, a warm path, a current market/company trigger, or a genuinely useful asset. More space is allowed only when each section adds new value. Lead with the recipient's world, organize distinct topics for a skim-reader, build from external context to specific proof, and place the offer after relevance is earned.

Do not apply cold-email brevity mechanically to a thoughtful warm note, or long-form depth to a cold first step.

## Sequence jobs

- **Step 1, relevance:** specific observation, one problem, one relevance/proof signal, lowest-friction CTA.
- **Step 2, new value:** add a new statistic, mechanism, proof point, or useful asset; never send a disguised “checking in.”
- **Step 3, honest bump:** acknowledge timing may be wrong, make one final easy offer, leave the door open.
- **Optional close:** close the loop without guilt, false scarcity, or passive aggression.

Every step must stand alone and use a distinct reason to reply. Escalate directness, not desperation.

## Proof, links, and CTA

One specific, authorised outcome beats several vague claims. Separate fact, inference, owner-provided claim, and assumption. Never invent recipient context, customer results, numbers, or first-person experience.

Use at most one link in a cold email. Earn the click by stating the time investment and specific value. A named-prospect note may layer a low-friction asset, diagnostic/audit, and conversation only when the evidence and context have earned each step.

Match posture to region and seniority using current evidence, not stereotypes. Region may affect warmth, directness, proof emphasis, and relationship runway; it never changes truth, privacy, consent, or suppression requirements.

## Acceptance checks

- The opening concerns the recipient's world, not the product or a generic compliment.
- The hook is specific to this role/company/trigger or explicitly labelled as a hypothesis.
- Each sequence step has a different job and value contribution.
- Proof is authorised and outcome-framed.
- The product appears only after relevance.
- The CTA matches the relationship and sequence step.
- Targeting, copy, provider execution, and send authority remain separate.
