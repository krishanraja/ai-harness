# Evidence and ethics

## Evidence ladder

1. Production randomised or strong quasi-experimental evidence for this product.
2. Product-specific cohort, funnel, qualitative, and usability evidence.
3. Primary research or official platform guidance with a relevant population and context.
4. Documented external case study with disclosed method and outcome.
5. Public practitioner pattern or observed implementation.

The bundled outcome records begin at level 5. They are hypotheses until stronger evidence is attached, and stronger evidence is attached only as rows in `evidence-log.jsonl` (see `evidence-loop.md`). The catalog itself never changes level.

## Claim discipline

- A visible pattern proves implementation, not effectiveness.
- A retained-user correlation suggests a candidate activation action, not causality.
- A movement after release is not attributable without a credible design.
- Do not transfer benchmark numbers across products without population and definition compatibility.
- Record contrary evidence and null results.

## Reject outright

- False scarcity, countdowns, social proof, or activity.
- Hidden price, renewal, limits, consent, or cancellation conditions.
- Forced continuity, obstruction, confirm-shaming, or disguised advertising.
- Permission requests unrelated to a visible user benefit.
- Contact upload or invitation without informed control.
- Guilt, loss threats, or punishment for pausing a routine.
- Fake processing delays presented as real work.
- Accessibility regressions traded for conversion.

A deadline, delay, social signal, or scarcity claim is acceptable only when true, material, disclosed, and not used to overpower informed choice.
