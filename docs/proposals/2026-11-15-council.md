# Council candidate, 2026-11-15

## Finding and correct layer

Control Center now runs a council (control-center plan item A3): five seats, each a lane of judgment, judge the daily move and the ideas Krish says into the one door, and Marcus, his chief of staff, alone makes the call. A session working for Krish anywhere else has no way to put a decision or an idea to that same council. Without a shared definition each session would invent its own seats and its own tests, and Krish would correct the same drift in more than one place, which is the failure the North Star's number counts.

The correct layer is a narrow skill: a read-only validator with a distinct trigger, a fixed workflow and an owned standard. It is staged as an inactive candidate under `candidates/council/`, outside the release builder, which reads `skills/` only.

This moves two parts of the North Star: a council defined once and held to one wording is a correction learned once, and a council read any session can run takes judgment work off Krish's hours while leaving the decision with him.

## What the candidate is

- `SKILL.md`: the trigger, the exclusions with their routes, the inputs and the stop conditions.
- `references/seats.md` and `references/seats.json`: the five seats (Offer, Attention, Experience, Scale, Distribution), each with its brief, agrees, clashes, asks and default action, copied from control-center `src/lib/councilSeats.ts` (`COUNCIL_SEATS`).
- `references/idea-tests.md` and `references/idea-tests.json`: the eight idea tests, each with its label and brief, copied from the `IDEA_TESTS` in control-center `api/_councilRead.ts`.
- `references/protocol.md`: how a read runs, in its own words. Krish's eight decision rules are read live from `krish-principles` and never copied in.
- `scripts/validate.py` and `scripts/tests/`: the gate, which the harness runner already calls for any candidate that carries them.

## One wording, two places

The canon's one-system rule says a fact has one home and every copy is a defect. Control-center is the home of the seats and the tests. The candidate keeps the same words because a session may not be able to reach control-center, and holds them to the home with a gate rather than by hand:

- the JSON was produced by reading control-center's source mechanically, and a second reader (Node evaluating the same source) agreed field for field;
- `scripts/validate.py` compares every copied field with control-center byte for byte whenever the two source files are present (a `control-center` folder beside this repository, `COUNCIL_CONTROL_CENTER`, or `--control-center <path>`), and fails on the first difference, naming the seat or test and the field;
- the two Markdown files must be exactly what the JSON draws (`python scripts/council.py render`), so the text a session reads cannot drift from the text the gate checks;
- where control-center is absent (the Windows CI runner), the comparison says it was skipped; it never claims a match it did not make.

**One open difference.** The first sentence of the `hard_to_copy` brief in control-center shares an eight-word run with an outside reference and so cannot enter this public repository under the 2026-11-15 ruling recorded in this change's commit body. The candidate carries a new first sentence in its place; the rest of that brief, and every other field, is control-center's text unchanged. The gate fails against control-center until control-center adopts the same sentence, which is the only change needed there: replace the opening question of the `hard_to_copy` brief in `api/_councilRead.ts` (the sentence before "If so, what does Krish hold") with

> Could one skilled builder make a working copy of this within a few days, using the AI tools available now?

The old sentence is not quoted here on purpose. Once control-center carries the new one, the gate matches byte for byte and the two places are one wording again.

## Missing-skill test

1. Does the need recur or carry consequence? Yes. Decisions and ideas reach Krish daily, and the council's call shapes where his hours go.
2. Is the trigger distinct? Yes, and deliberately narrow: only an explicit ask for the council or a council read, or a named seat. A general "should I" stays with `krish-principles`.
3. Is there a repeatable workflow? Yes: frame, seats, dissenter, chair for a decision; tests, what has to be true, decision rules, seats, chair for an idea.
4. Is there an owned standard for good output? Yes: control-center's seats and tests, plus the protocol's rules (scores held at 6 on an assumption, no counting or averaging, the best case for the other side, no invented figure).
5. Can success be verified independently? Partly today. The gate checks the words deterministically; the behaviour suite is written for a judge that does not yet exist, as with every behaviour suite in this repository (`evals/README.md`).
6. Can its authority be bounded? Yes. It reads and judges; it writes nothing, sends nothing and spends nothing, and a "go" approves nothing.
7. Would a chapter in an existing skill be materially worse? Yes. Inside `krish-principles` it would fire on every "should I", which is the opposite of the narrow trigger Krish's council needs, and it would put five seats into an always-on skill's context.

## Overlap review

| Neighbour | What it owns | Where the council stops |
|---|---|---|
| `krish-principles` | his doctrine and decision rules, every "should I" | the council reads his rules live from it and fires only on an explicit council ask |
| `strategy-brief` | the route, authority and checks for material work | the council judges whether; planning how stays there, including the week's test |
| `decision-ledger` | recording a final decision with its rationale | the council never records; Krish's decision, with the dissent, is handed there |
| `krish-voice` | words sent or posted in his name | the council drafts nothing to send |
| `take-the-brief` | interviewing him before work starts | the council asks once for missing options, nothing more |
| `evidence-research` | outside facts and figures | the council quotes only what it is given and marks the rest unknown |
| `mindmake-os` | live Control Center state, including what its council already said | the council keeps no copy of Control Center's reads |
| `verification-loop`, `ctrl-check` | checking work and standards | the council judges a choice, not an artifact |

Functional overlap with any one neighbour is well under the standard's 20 percent; the closest, `krish-principles`, shares the decision rules, which the council reads and never restates.
